### [[haze]] ###
TASK: haze removal.

T1  Haze removal thoroughness: in every region where content is identifiable
    (including mid-ground and background elements that are still visible), haze is
    removed as thoroughly as possible — visibility and contrast are substantially
    recovered. A minimal, timid change is a FAILURE here; this task demands the
    best achievable dehazing, not a cautious one.
T2  Airlight veil eliminated: no residual whitish/greyish haze film remains over
    objects that are visible. Check buildings, roads, edges and textures
    specifically — a result that removed the bulk of the haze but left a milky
    film on visible content fails this item.
T3  Visible content reads clean and transparent, not washed-out: local contrast and
    texture are restored in the de-hazed regions, and the picture again has genuine
    blacks and genuine bright areas rather than everything compressed toward
    mid-grey. NOTE (2026-09-09): the result may well come out DARKER overall than
    the input — the haze was a bright white veil, and removing it removes that
    brightness. Darker-but-with-restored-tonal-range is CORRECT here; do not record
    it as a defect. What fails is the CONTENT staying pale and flat.
T4  OBSCURED SKY UNCHANGED (hard): if the sky is obscured by haze in the input, it
    stays obscured. It must NOT be turned into a clear blue sky with white clouds,
    nor otherwise fabricated. Fabricating sky content is a severe failure.
T5  Physical plausibility: dehazing has not produced impossible results — no
    unnaturally black shadows, no colour banding in the sky, no dark halos around
    edges, no localised "punched-through" patches.


### [[lowlight]] ###
TASK: low-light enhancement.

T1  Enhancement strength: dark-region content is substantially more visible than in
    the input. Clearly conservative, barely-brightened output is a FAILURE — this
    task demands strong enhancement.
T2  Shadow readability: detail that was present but buried in the shadows is now
    readable. Structures in dark areas are resolved, not merely lifted to grey.
T3  NIGHT ATMOSPHERE PRESERVED: the scene still reads as night or dusk. It must not
    be converted into a daylight-looking image. Brightening for readability is
    required; changing the time of day is a failure.
T4  No hallucination in the darkest regions: areas that carry genuinely no
    information in the input are left plausibly dark rather than filled with
    invented detail.
T5  Highlights preserved: light sources and bright areas are not blown out or
    clipped by the enhancement.
T6  No lifting artifacts: no grey-black "fog" from raising the black point, no
    unnatural HDR look, no halos around light sources, no colour blotches in the
    lifted shadows.
T7  COLOUR IDENTITY PRESERVED: brightness and saturation depth may change as a
    consequence of enhancement, but hue category must not shift — red objects stay
    red, yellow lights stay yellow. A hue-category shift is a failure.


### [[rain]] ###
TASK: rainy-scene restoration.

T1  Rain artifacts removed cleanly and as completely as reliably possible: visible
    rain streaks and raindrop artifacts are gone from the content. Obvious residual
    streaks or droplets on visible content are a FAILURE.
T2  Rain haze veil removed: the misty veil that reduced contrast is eliminated and
    visibility is recovered.
T3  Background clarity actively restored: where rain caused defocus, blur or loss of
    background clarity, the background is genuinely sharpened and made visible. A
    result that removed the rain but left the background blurred FAILS this item —
    de-raining alone is not sufficient.
T4  RAINY AMBIANCE PRESERVED: the scene still reads as a rainy scene, with its wet
    look and lighting mood. It must not be converted into a dry, sunny scene.
T5  COLOUR AND TONE UNCHANGED: no hue shift, no saturation shift, no white-balance
    change, no recolouring.
T6  No ghosting or smearing where rain was removed; fine edges are not
    over-smoothed in the process.


### [[snow]] ###
TASK: snowy-scene restoration.

T1  Falling snow removed cleanly and as completely as possible: airborne
    snowflakes and snow particles that occluded the view are gone. Obvious
    residual flakes over visible content are a FAILURE.
T2  Snow veil removed: the haze-like veil is eliminated and the occluded view is
    restored, with balanced contrast recovered.
T3  SETTLED SNOW IS CONTENT, NOT AN ARTIFACT: snow lying on the ground, roofs,
    branches, vehicles or any surface is part of the scene and must remain exactly
    as it is. Only airborne flakes and the atmospheric veil are to be removed.
    Clearing away settled snow is a content violation and a severe failure.
T4  No hallucination of what was hidden behind the flakes: uncovered areas are
    reconstructed only where evidence supports it.
T5  COLOUR AND TONE UNCHANGED: no hue shift, no white-balance shift, no
    recolouring.
T6  Global brightness character consistent with the input: clarity may improve, but
    the overall brightness/mood is not restyled.
T7  Edges and details recovered with balanced contrast; no harsh over-sharpening.


### [[mix]] ###
TASK: mixed realistic degradation — the general-purpose restoration case
(noise / compression / blur, plus exposure and lighting artifacts).

T1  Noise removed cleanly: sensor and read noise, and colour blotches, are
    suppressed without oversmoothing texture away.
T2  Compression artifacts removed: blocking, ringing and mosquito noise around
    high-contrast edges are eliminated.
T3  Blur resolved: the softness the CAPTURE introduced — defocus of the intended
    subject, camera shake, general mush — is genuinely deblurred and detail
    recovered, not merely masked by sharpening. Shallow depth-of-field background
    blur is EXCLUDED: bokeh produced by the lens aperture is a property of the
    photograph, and leaving it in place is correct, not a failure (2026-08-29).
T4  Improvement is not confined to one spot: the degradations named above are
    addressed wherever they occur, rather than only in the main subject while
    comparably degraded areas elsewhere are left untouched. A background that
    stays soft because it was shot out of focus does NOT fail this item.
T5  Overexposure handled: where highlights are blown out, recoverable detail is
    restored and the region no longer reads as a flat white patch. Detail that
    carries no information in the input is not invented (see U2).
T6  Reflections and glare reduced: specular reflections on glass or water, veiling
    glare and light-source halos that obscure content are suppressed, and the
    content behind them becomes readable where evidence supports it.
T7  COLOUR CAST NOT CORRECTED: any colour cast in the input remains, at the same
    strength. A cast may be intentional, so correcting it is out of scope here.
    Colour and tone otherwise unchanged: no stylised recolouring, no hue category
    shift, no white-balance change.
T8  No hallucination in uncertain regions: heavily damaged areas are left
    plausibly unresolved rather than invented.
