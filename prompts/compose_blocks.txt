### [[haze]] ###
TASK-SPECIFIC GOAL: haze removal.

STATE THE TARGET AS A CHECKABLE END STATE, NOT AS AN ADVERB (2026-08-30).
Writing "aggressive"/"thorough"/"maximal" does not make the editor act more
strongly — measured across 100 images, instructions loaded with such words
produced no more change than instructions without them. What works is telling
the editor what the finished picture must LOOK like, so it can check itself.
Put these end states in the instruction, in concrete terms tied to this image:

  * NO WHITE FILM ANYWHERE. After restoration there is no milky/greyish layer
    sitting over any surface. A viewer comparing input and output must be able to
    say the veil is gone, not merely reduced. Name the surfaces it currently sits
    on in this image.
  * DISTANT CONTENT AS LEGIBLE AS NEAR CONTENT. Edges, textures and boundaries of
    mid-ground and background objects must be as clearly readable as foreground
    ones — not "improved", but actually resolvable. Name the distant objects.
  * THE CONTENT COMES OUT MORE VIVID, NOT WASHED OUT. Haze desaturates and flattens
    everything toward pale grey; the restored surfaces must show their own real
    colours at full strength and their own light-to-dark variation. A result that
    is merely a slightly cleaner version of the same pale image has failed.
    (The overall picture may well end up darker than the input — that is correct,
    because the bright white veil is being removed. What must not happen is the
    CONTENT staying pale and flat.)
  * FULL TONAL RANGE RESTORED. The haze has compressed everything toward mid-grey;
    the output should again have genuine blacks and genuine bright areas rather
    than a flat mid-tone image.

MUST NOT (state explicitly in the instruction):
  * If the sky is obscured by haze, it stays unchanged. It must NOT be turned into
    a clear blue sky with white clouds, nor otherwise fabricated. Name the sky's
    actual current state from the report when writing this.
  * No artifacts from aggressive dehazing: no unnaturally black shadows, no colour
    banding in the sky, no dark halos around edges, no localised punched-through
    patches. These remain hard limits — the strength above is about removing the
    veil completely, never about pushing contrast until it breaks.

NOTE ON COLOUR: this is the one task type where colour and brightness necessarily
change — recovering contrast and transparency alters them. Do not impose a
"colour must stay identical" requirement here. Still avoid stylised recolouring.


### [[lowlight]] ###
TASK-SPECIFIC GOAL: low-light enhancement.

STATE THE TARGET AS A CHECKABLE END STATE, NOT AS AN ADVERB (2026-08-30).
"STRONGER"/"not conservative" did not produce stronger results — measured over
100 images the dark regions were lifted by almost nothing. Describe what the
finished picture must LOOK like instead:

  * DARK CONTENT BECOMES READABLE AT NORMAL VIEWING. Objects currently lost in the
    shadows must be identifiable without zooming in or adjusting a display. Name
    the specific dark regions in this image and what is in them.
  * NO REGION STAYS A FEATURELESS BLACK MASS. Areas that hold recoverable
    structure must show that structure — shapes, edges and surfaces separable from
    one another rather than a single flat dark patch. (Areas genuinely holding no
    information stay dark; see MUST NOT.)
  * SHADOW DETAIL, NOT JUST A BRIGHTER PICTURE. The lift must open up the shadows
    specifically. Uniformly raising the whole image, which washes it out while
    leaving the shadows just as closed, is a failure of this task.
  * THE LIFT IS SUBSTANTIAL AND VISIBLE. Comparing input and output, a viewer must
    immediately see that dark areas opened up. A change only detectable by
    measurement is under-enhancement.
  * Suppression of the noise and colour blotches that the lift will reveal, and
    clarity/sharpness recovery for the newly visible structures, without
    oversmoothing them away.

MUST NOT (state explicitly):
  * Realistic night/dusk atmosphere must be preserved — the scene must still read
    as night or dusk, never converted to a daylight look. Opening the shadows is
    not the same as making the picture look like day: the bright/dark relationship
    of the scene stays intact.
  * No hallucinated detail in severely dark or uncertain regions; leave them
    plausibly dark instead.
  * Highlights preserved: no over-exposure or clipping of light sources.
  * No grey-black lifting artifacts, no unnatural HDR look, no halos around lights.
    These remain hard limits — the strength above is about opening the shadows,
    never about pushing until the image looks tone-mapped.
  * COLOUR IDENTITY: brightness and saturation depth may change as a consequence of
    enhancement, but hue category must not shift — red objects stay red, yellow
    lights stay yellow.


### [[rain]] ###
TASK-SPECIFIC GOAL: rainy-scene restoration.

Require in the instruction:
  * Clean and complete removal of visible rain streaks, raindrop artifacts and the
    rain haze veil, as thoroughly as can be done reliably — no obvious residual
    rain artifacts left on visible content.
  * If rain streaks/droplets caused background defocus, blur or reduced background
    clarity: ACTIVE restoration of background sharpness and visibility. Removing
    the rain while leaving the background blurred is not acceptable — say so.
  * Recovery of contrast, visibility and textures.
  * Denoising and clarity/sharpness recovery for ALL regions, not only rain removal.

MUST NOT (state explicitly):
  * Rainy weather ambiance and lighting mood are preserved — the scene must not
    look dry or sunny. Wet-surface cues present in the image stay.
  * Colour and overall tone unchanged: no hue shift, no saturation shift, no
    white-balance change, no recolouring.
  * No ghosting or smearing where rain was removed; no over-smoothing of fine edges.


### [[snow]] ###
TASK-SPECIFIC GOAL: snowy-scene restoration.

Require in the instruction:
  * Clean and as-complete-as-possible removal of snow artifacts that OCCLUDE the
    view: airborne snowflakes and snow particles in the air, and the haze-like snow
    veil. Name where visibility is most impaired.
  * Denoising and clarity/sharpness recovery for ALL regions, not only snow
    suppression.
  * Recovery of edges, details and balanced contrast, without harsh oversharpening.

MUST NOT (state explicitly):
  * SETTLED SNOW IS SCENE CONTENT: snow lying on the ground, roofs, branches,
    vehicles or any other surface must remain exactly as it is. Only airborne
    flakes and the atmospheric veil are removed. Name the settled snow present in
    this image so it is unambiguous.
  * No hallucination of what was hidden behind the flakes.
  * Global colour and overall tone unchanged: no hue shift, no white-balance shift,
    no recolouring.
  * Global brightness character stays consistent with the input while clarity
    improves.


### [[mix]] ###
TASK-SPECIFIC GOAL: mixed realistic degradation — the general-purpose restoration
case (noise / compression / blur, plus exposure and lighting artifacts).

Require in the instruction:
  * Fidelity first: exact scene content, geometry, position and structure preserved.
  * Clean removal of noise and compression artifacts — sensor/read noise, colour
    blotches, blocking, ringing, mosquito noise around high-contrast edges — and
    genuine recovery of detail and clarity for visible structures.
  * Genuine deblurring of the softness the CAPTURE introduced — defocus of the
    intended subject, camera shake, general mush — with detail recovered rather than
    masked by sharpening. Shallow depth-of-field blur is NOT in that set: background
    bokeh produced by the lens aperture is a property of the photograph and stays as
    it is (2026-08-29 change).
  * Clarity improvement where the image is degraded. Name the regions where each
    degradation is worst. Do not require that every region reach the same sharpness —
    a correctly restored photograph may still have an out-of-focus background.
  * Where highlights are blown out: recovery of the detail that is still
    recoverable, so the region no longer reads as a flat white patch.
  * Where specular reflections (on glass or water), veiling glare or light-source
    halos obscure content: suppression of them and recovery of the content behind,
    to the extent the input supports it. Name where they are.

MUST NOT (state explicitly):
  * COLOUR CAST IS NOT CORRECTED: if the image carries a colour cast, it stays at
    the same strength — the cast may be intentional. Global colour and overall tone
    otherwise unchanged: no stylised recolouring, no hue category shift, no
    white-balance change.
  * No invented objects: do not add, remove or replace things that are not in the
    input.
  * No oversmoothing that erases genuine texture along with the noise.
